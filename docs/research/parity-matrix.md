## How to read the labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **REQUIRED V1** | Claude Ship does this. Code-Shipper v1 must do the equivalent to claim parity, and the pack already specifies it (FR-*, NFR-*, D*, K*, or REVISION-2 §2-§3). No new scope is created by this label. |
| **INTENTIONAL DIFFERENCE** | v1 deliberately behaves differently. The rationale is in the "Differences we own" list at the end, or in the row's note. |
| **FOLLOW-UP** | A real gap that is not in v1 scope. Needs an owner decision before it is added; several are marked P0 by the research pass. |
| **UNVERIFIED** | Claude Ship's exact behaviour is not established from the pages read in this pass, or is version-dependent. Do not infer it, and do not claim parity or divergence on it. |

## The matrix

| 2 | **Triggers.** Comment commands: `@claude review` starts one review, `@claude review always` also subscribes the PR to push-triggered reviews, `@claude review stop` ends that subscription. | 7 | **Review tiers.** Quick, Standard (default) and Deep. An Owner sets the organization default and can give one repository its own level. More thorough costs more. | 8 | **Quick review semantics.** Quick is "a lighter pass. The fastest level, at the lowest cost." | Quick reduces the number of lenses only. | 9 | **Verification.** A verification step checks candidate findings against the code and filters out false positives before they are reported. | Every candidate finding goes through a pass that tries to refute it from the code; unverified findings are dropped, not posted (FR-R2). | 10 | **Review is advisory.** A review never approves or blocks the pull request. | 11 | **Severity vocabulary.** Exactly three levels with fixed markers: Important ("a bug that should be fixed before merging"), Nit ("worth fixing but not blocking"), Pre-existing ("exists in the codebase but wasn't introduced by this PR"). | The same three, with the same meanings (FR-R3). | REQUIRED V1 | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review-pull-requests | Matches exactly. | 12 | **Per-finding rationale.** Where the review recorded its reasoning, a finding carries a collapsed **Why this was flagged** section with why it was flagged and how the problem was verified. | Structured evidence is kept in the finding record; nothing user-facing beyond the finding text. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review-pull-requests | Cheapest credibility upgrade available. | 13 | **Posting fallbacks.** A finding whose inline comment GitHub rejected appears as an annotation in the **Files changed** tab, and every finding appears in the check run's **Details** table sorted by severity. | Inline comments plus one summary comment (FR-R4). | 14 | **Machine-readable verdict.** The check run's `output.text` carries a severity line that scripts can read with `gh api … --jq`; a missing line must be treated as no verdict, not as zero findings, and a kept-earlier-result title also has no severity line. | A commit status `code-shipper/approvals` whose description states who still has to approve (K5). | 15 | **Re-review scope.** A later automatic review covers what changed since the last review, posts fewer nits and pre-existing findings, and states the count it withheld ("outside the scope of this re-review and not posted"). | Every head is reviewed in full (NFR-3 exact head). | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review-pull-requests | The disclosure of what was withheld is the trust-critical half, not just the cost saving. |
| 16 | **Result reuse.** After a push that adds and removes the same lines, with no Important finding still open, Code Review keeps the earlier result instead of spending a review. | No reuse; a new head is reviewed. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review-pull-requests | Cost and noise control. | 17 | **Review instructions source.** `REVIEW.md` is read from the base or default branch and never from the pull request's own changes. | The same rule, for `REVIEW.md` and for our own `.code-shipper/review.md` (FR-R5). | REQUIRED V1 | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review | Parity. | 19 | **Review is read-only and does not run your code.** Inside a review, repository access is read-only, no commits are pushed, and the review works from the diff and surrounding code without building or running it. | Read-only for the reviewer role, no push capability (FR-R4, NFR-1). | 20 | **Fix loop work items.** Failing check (only when the cause is in code the PR touches), merge conflict (merge base in and resolve), a person's review comment (small specific change plus reply, or a reply alone when the request is large or about design), a review bot's finding (Code Review Important; other bots' lint findings and bugs), and the **Claude Approvals** check where it waits on something a commit can fix. | The first four (FR-S1). | 21 | **Flaky checks.** A failed job is re-run once under defined conditions. | No re-run capability; the broker has no re-run operation. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/pr-steward | Many "failing checks" are flakes; without re-run, the fixer rewrites code to chase a non-bug. | 22 | **Base-branch handling.** A merge conflict is resolved by merging the base branch in. | 23 | **Generated files.** Lockfiles and generated files are regenerated rather than hand-edited. | The diff-scope check would likely refuse a regenerated lockfile as out of scope. | UNVERIFIED | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/pr-steward | The regeneration behaviour was recorded by the research pass from `pr-steward-pull-requests`, which was not re-read today. | 24 | **Watch state on the PR.** While Steward watches, the PR carries `claude-pr-steward-watching`; Steward pushes commits and replies to comments without asking first. | 25 | **Stop persistence.** Stopping is durable: it does not restart on later pushes or comments, and restarting needs an explicit command or the label. | Stop is specified; "stays stopped across later pushes" is not stated as a guarantee. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/pr-steward | A loop that restarts itself after a stop is the fastest way to lose an operator's trust. | 27 | **One agent per PR.** Auto-fix on a PR that PR Steward is watching fails, so two agents cannot work the same PR. | A per-PR ledger and lock, but no stated rule that another agent (including another Code-Shipper process) must not work the same PR. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/pr-steward | Matters for us specifically: our watcher, a user's `/ship` and a cron tick could all run on one PR. Worth one sentence in the spec. |
| 28 | **Policy file location.** `.claude/approvals.yml` at the repository root on the default branch; `.claude/approvals.yaml` is read only where the `.yml` file is absent. | `.code-shipper/approvals.yml` on the default branch only, own format, explicitly not compatible with Claude's (K2). | REQUIRED V1 | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy | Deliberate format divergence, recorded in K2. An importer can come later; claiming schema compatibility now would be false. |
| 29 | **Policy change inside a PR.** While a policy change is open, Approvals can check the changed file for errors and applies none of its rules; the default branch decides. The result shows as a "Policy files in this change" section and a failed check titled "This change breaks a policy file". | The rule is right: a policy change is reported, never applied (tech-spec §4.1). There is no visible validation surface. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy | The missing half is the pre-merge "does my policy parse, and here is the message" loop, which is also the main authoring pain. Research ranked this P0. |
| 30 | **Nested policies.** A `.claude/approvals.yml` inside a directory governs files under it. For each changed file, Approvals walks up; the nearest enforcing file that covers the file, and in which a rule with a verdict applies, decides. `nested: inherit` counts as that file's rule, `paths` are relative to the directory, `locals` reach down, and each file has its own mode, so a root file in observe mode contributes nothing under a child's enforcing file. Research ranked it P0; it is out of v1 scope unless the owner says otherwise. |
| 31 | **Matching vocabulary.** Three distinct relationships: *matches* (paths include the file, no `!` item takes it out), *applies* (matches, `mode: enforce`, and `if` true or absent), *covers* (matches, enforce, and has a verdict whatever `if` says). Where rules cover a file and none applies, the check fails. | Strictest-wins across applicable rules; the matches/applies/covers triple is not modelled. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy | Without the triple, "no rule applies to this file" is ambiguous, and the fail-closed property cannot be proven. Research ranked it P0. |
| 32 | **Combination.** Approvals takes the strictest verdict of the rules that apply: `blocks` outranks `comments`, which outranks `approves`. No `max_age`. | REQUIRED V1 | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy | Parity on the property that matters — a catch-all rule cannot be relaxed by a narrower one. `max_age` is a follow-up. |
| 33 | **Ordered overrides.** A rule carries an `override` list read from the top; the first true override replaces the rule's verdict. | 34 | **Rule keys.** Each rule needs `purpose`, `paths`, and at least one of `verdict`, `advisory`, `findings`, `reuse`; a rule with no verdict asks nobody and covers no file. `seats` is a count from 1 to 16 plus a `from` people list; `claude` is `approves`, `comments` or `blocks`. | 35 | **Built-in classifiers.** `claude/code_review` answers `pass` or `fail` (and `.severity` reads `none`, `minimal` or `high`); `claude/diff` answers `trivial`, `same_intent` or `different` and is read under `reuse`. | 36 | **Custom classifiers.** `.claude/classifiers/<name>/def.yml` up to 64 KiB, referenced from a rule as `repo/<name>`, with typed `outputs`, `inputs` (including a `merge` rule for disagreements), `tools` grants and a `budget` (default cap 10 US dollars a run). | Absent. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-classifiers | Research recommends refusing classifiers for v1 and recording the refusal in an ADR. | 37 | **Approval survival.** After a push that changes the diff, earlier approvals normally stop counting; where the policy writes `reuse`, some continue, typically gated on `claude/diff <= same_intent`. The `!pr/authors` hazard only matters if reuse is ever added; record it now so a later implementer does not rediscover it. |
| 38 | **Finding resolution.** `findings` with a `resolution` list says who may close a finding, by resolving the thread or commenting `@claude approvals resolve <ids>`. There is no resolution authority, so clearing an Important finding requires a new review. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy , https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-classifiers | Also the source of the warning to keep resolution lists short: a wide list lets someone resolve `.severity` and pass a failed review with nothing fixed. |
| 39 | **Author seat.** On a PR opened by someone with write access, the author fills one seat without approving, in a set that admits them. Who holds the seat is defined per commit, and nobody holds it after a commit one person wrote and another pushed. Bots fill it only in a set that admits them. A machine user (an agent under a non-`claude` account) holds it as a person does, and can be the one person a protected file needs. | `seats.from` lists may include authors, writers and agents, with exclusions (tech-spec §4.1); the approver must differ from the author and the fixer (FR-A4). | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy | Mechanism differs: Claude grants an automatic seat credit with a defined holder and defined gaps, ours is opt-in list membership. | 40 | **Counting approvals.** An approval counts from a GitHub review or an `@claude approve` comment, on the latest commit or an earlier one that still counts. | 41 | **Protected files.** Built-in list: every policy file, everything under `.claude/classifiers/`, `REVIEW.md` at the repository root, `.gitmodules`, `.gitattributes`, symlinks, submodule pointers, and any file named `CODEOWNERS`. `.github/`, `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `.mcp.json` and the rest of `.claude/` are not protected. | A broader list (FR-A2) that also covers CI workflow files, review instructions, agent instruction files, `.gitmodules`, `.gitattributes`, symlinks and submodule pointers. | REQUIRED V1 | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy | Ours is stricter, and defensibly so for a vendor-neutral tool: `.github/` and agent instruction files are exactly what a PR could use to weaken review. | 42 | **Absolute vetoes.** Claude never approves a PR that changes a `.gitattributes` file, a symlink or a submodule pointer, whatever the rules say. | Protected files require a person; there is no unconditional agent veto for those three file kinds. | FOLLOW-UP | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy | These three can redirect where code is later read from, so "a person must approve" is weaker than "the agent may never approve". | 43 | **Who may be the person.** The person Approvals asks for on a protected file can be the PR's author. | Protected files always need a person, and agent approval additionally requires the approver to differ from both the author and the fixer (FR-A2, FR-A4). | INTENTIONAL DIFFERENCE | https://code.claude.com/docs/en/approvals-policy | Ours is stricter and matches D1's separation intent. ## Differences we own

Deliberate v1 divergences from Claude Ship. Each one is a position, not an accident, and each
should be stated in the README or SECURITY.md rather than left for a reader to infer.

- **Harness-neutral.** Reviews and fixes run on the user's existing harness account — Hermes,
  Codex, Claude Code, OpenCode — behind one adapter interface, with a direct-API adapter as an
  option (D3, FR-H1). Claude Ship only works inside Claude. This is the primary reason the project
  exists, and it is the difference a user notices first.
- **Hermes-first integration.** A `/ship` slash command, a `hermes code-shipper` CLI subcommand and
  a script-only cron job for repo watching (FR-X1, F6). Hermes is the only harness whose scheduler,
  plugin seams and Desktop surface we can integrate deeply, so it gets first-class treatment and
  the others get contract-tested adapters.
- **CLI, TUI and Desktop as first-class surfaces.** One shared state, event schema and
  authenticated action service behind all three, with no merge control on any of them (FR-P1,
  REVISION-2 §5). Claude Ship's local flow is a terminal prompt inside one session, and its Desktop
  surface for Ship has no published reference to copy (row 60). Ours is a keyboard-driven PR inbox
  with diff, findings, checks, policy and activity views, and a Hermes Desktop page plus contextual
  pane — built to Hermes idioms because there is nothing to imitate.
- **MIT, and no plan gate.** Claude Ship is Team or Enterprise only and is structurally unavailable
  to organizations under Zero Data Retention, HIPAA, CMEK, FERPA or Access Transparency (D9,
  row 61). An MIT local tool costs nothing to try, needs no Owner role, and can serve the buyers
  Anthropic excludes. No compliance claim follows from that; the absence of a vendor plan is not a
  certification.
- **Authority by credential separation and broker, not by refusing commands.** The model and any
  repository code it runs never hold a GitHub credential; every write goes through a broker with a
  per-role allowlist, exact-head checks and revalidation (NFR-1, ADR 0002, row 47). Claude's own
  docs concede that text rules plus a model check can be passed by a differently-written command.
  Our M0 spike showed a Contents-write token merging an unprotected PR, so we describe what
  enforces the boundary and do not claim a token is incapable.
- **A decoupled watcher.** Scheduled, resumable and unattended, with the worker on an exact-head
  snapshot rather than the user's checkout (D5, row 58). Claude's watch dies with the terminal
  session and cannot be scripted at all (row 57). For a user who wants fixes to land while they
  are elsewhere, this is the whole point.
- **Fail-closed publication and budgets.** The first commit, push and PR need explicit
  confirmation; silence is never consent; no flag disables the broker or the secret scan; unknown
  spend is reported as unknown and a run is refused when the cap cannot be enforced (FR-T1, FR-S4,
  rows 55, 62). Claude's local default is to proceed after a few seconds.
- **Stricter protected paths and separation.** A wider protected-file list that includes CI
  workflow files, review instructions and agent instruction files; the approver must differ from
  both the author and the fixer; and observe mode can never block (FR-A2, FR-A4, rows 41, 43, 44).
  Some of this is stricter than Claude, and one part — a solo maintainer cannot self-approve a
  policy change — is a usability trade we should name.
- **No classifiers in v1 unless the owner says otherwise.** No model judgement inside the policy
  engine, because a classifier reads text the PR author wrote and can be steered, which contradicts
  NFR-2 (rows 35, 36, 50). This is the clearest place where we choose a smaller feature set over a
  wider attack surface, and the ADR should say so in exactly those terms.
- **A grammar of our own, deliberately not Claude-compatible.** `.code-shipper/approvals.yml` with
  dot-style facts and an ordered-override rule shape that keeps the strict verdict on the rule
  (K2, rows 28, 33, 34). Imitating the schema would imply a compatibility we cannot test; an
  importer can be written later if it is worth it.
